# Family: `crates/app/` — assembly & enforcement

**Layer(s):** `app` (unchanged for three of four crates; `ironclaw_config` reassigns `substrates` → `app` — the one layer delta in this family, confirmed against its current `Cargo.toml` and not among the six reassignments PROPOSAL §1/§8.1 names by title) · **Crates (target):** 4 — `ironclaw_composition` (renamed from `ironclaw_reborn_composition`, radically narrowed) · `ironclaw_cli` (directory renamed from `ironclaw_reborn_cli`; package name stays `ironclaw`) · `ironclaw_config` (renamed from `ironclaw_reborn_config`, vendor sections removed) · `ironclaw_architecture` · plus `tools/ironclaw_stress`, `tools/ironclaw_silk_decoder`, root `fuzz/`, and the workspace-root package `ironclaw_reborn_integration_tests` (see the closing subsection) · **Security posture:** holds no standing authority beyond deployment-mode *selection* (never policy content) and fail-closed readiness gating. Its one deliberate privileged act is narrow and named: `ironclaw_cli` is the only crate in the entire workspace permitted to name a concrete extension package (the DEL-7 rule), and it alone implements the `AdminApiTokenMinter` inversion port composition defines but does not satisfy.

Source authority: `docs/reborn/target-architecture/PROPOSAL.md` §5, §6.10, §7, §8, §9 rows 64–67 and 69–73, §11.2/§11.4, §12.2, §12.10. Live-code citations were re-verified against the worktree this session (`dde662d5a` baseline); re-check file:line before acting on them.

## Identity — what this family IS

`app/` is the assembly root, the shipped artifact, the boot-config leaf, and the enforcement suite — four crates whose only shared trait is that nothing else in the workspace may depend on any of them. `ironclaw_reborn_composition` wires every owner crate from every other family into a running `RebornRuntime`; `ironclaw` (the CLI binary, directory `ironclaw_reborn_cli`) is the thing an operator actually runs, and the one place concrete extension packages get named; `ironclaw_reborn_config` is the boot-time `config.toml` schema everything else in this family reads; `ironclaw_architecture` is pure test code that fails the build when any *other* crate's dependency graph or public surface drifts from the declared model.

This is also the family where line counts invert the usual expectation. Composition alone is 76,984 production-tree lines plus a separate 27,856-line `tests/` directory — more than either of the other two families combined. The CLI is 25,538 src + 7,328 tests. Config is a comparatively tiny 4,284 src + 437 tests. And `ironclaw_architecture` has **zero** production lines — its `lib.rs` is four lines of doc comment declaring "this crate intentionally has no production dependencies" (`crates/ironclaw_architecture/src/lib.rs:1-4`) — against 10,407 lines spread across 17 test files. Three of these four crates carry more test mass than product mass, and the fourth *is* test mass. That is not incidental: it is the direct consequence of this family's job being enforcement and wiring rather than domain behavior, and it is why PROPOSAL §11.4 singles out `ironclaw_architecture` as the crate every other family's "must never" claim ultimately cashes out through.

The headline fact this family document has to reconcile: composition today is **not** primarily an assembly crate. By the audit's own production-line measurement it runs roughly 30% assembly / 70% behavior (PROPOSAL §2.4, ~46.6k production lines), and this session's direct module inventory (below) confirms it module-by-module — `runtime/local_dev/**` alone is over 20,000 lines, and it is not a "local dev" fallback at all: `RuntimeSubstrate` (`crates/ironclaw_reborn_composition/src/deployment.rs:87-92`) has exactly two variants, `None` and `ProductionShaped`, and `runtime.rs:3647` hardcodes `let local_runtime = Some(&services);` unconditionally. The name is a historical artifact; the tree it names is the production capability/delivery/approval/skill path. Narrowing composition to what §6.10.1 actually charters it to keep is the largest single move in either family document.

## What makes it distinct

**Against every other family, the same asymmetry holds: they are bounded, `app` is not.** Every family document in this series names an explicit allowed-dependency set — contracts may see only itself, substrate may see contracts, domains may see substrate/events/contracts, kernel may see domains/events/substrate/contracts, and so on up the ladder. `app`'s allowed-dependency cell in PROPOSAL §8.2's forbidden-edge matrix is simply **"any"** — the only family for which that is true, and the direct consequence of criterion 1 ("independent contract") applied to an assembly root: composition is *defined* as the one crate allowed to see everything, with `composition_public_api_is_service_shaped` and the composition mass ratchet (PR #6167) standing in for the deny-list every other family gets instead.

Seeing everything is not the same as owning everything, and this is the line the family repeatedly crosses today and must not cross in the target:

- vs `contracts/`: contracts define neutral vocabulary; `app` defines nothing neutral — it selects and constructs concrete implementations of vocabulary defined elsewhere.
- vs `substrate/`/`domains/`: those families own mechanism and record grammar; `app` never owns a record shape — `runtime/local_dev/**`'s current skill-activation and outbound-delivery logic (below) is exactly this line violated, and its shed destinations are `skills`/`outbound`, not a composition-owned equivalent.
- vs `kernel/`: kernel makes authority decisions; `app` selects *which* policy applies (`DeploymentMode`, `RuntimeProfile`) but must never author policy *content* — `profile_approval_authorization.rs`'s 1,824 lines of actual approval-policy logic living in composition today is this line violated, and it sheds to `approvals`/`authorization`/`runtime_policy`.
- vs `lanes/`/`loop/`: those own execution mechanics; `app` only registers which implementation a deployment uses.
- vs `extensions/`: the registry/host/manager own extension lifecycle; `app`'s only extension-shaped privilege is the CLI's binding table, and even that is *data* (`ChannelExtensionBinding`) the generic extension host consumes — the CLI never re-implements extension lifecycle logic itself.
- vs `product/`: product owns conversation UX and delivery semantics; `app`'s `automation/` (6,110 ln), `admin_user_directory.rs` (322 ln), and `root/communication_context.rs` (928 ln) are exactly this line violated today — all three shed to `product`.

**The binary is the only crate naming concrete extension packages.** `crates/ironclaw_reborn_cli/src/runtime/native_extensions.rs` (232 ln) is the one file in the entire workspace that imports `ironclaw_slack_extension` and `ironclaw_telegram_extension` by name and builds their `ChannelExtensionBinding`s; `crates/ironclaw_reborn_cli/src/first_party/{gsuite.rs,web_access.rs}` are the one place that names `ironclaw_first_party_extensions`' concrete registrar types. `ironclaw_reborn_composition::input::ChannelExtensionBinding` (`input.rs:234-243`) receives those bindings as opaque `Arc<dyn ChannelAdapter>` values — its own doc comment says it plainly: "composition itself never names a concrete extension crate." This is `concrete_extension_crates_link_only_from_the_binary_and_tests`, already mechanically enforced, and it is the cleanest boundary in this entire family — the one place where "app sees everything" narrows to "except the binary is the *only* one allowed to see extension packages by name."

**"Composition wires owners, never becomes one."** That is the family's one-sentence charter (echoing `crates/ironclaw_reborn_composition/CLAUDE.md:1-3`, "Own only top-level Reborn composition for production/app startup… Keep lower substrate handles private to factories and owning crates"), and it is the test every shed item in the `ironclaw_composition` crate spec below fails today and must pass in the target: if a module in `crates/ironclaw_reborn_composition/src/` computes a policy decision, renders a prompt, or owns a domain record shape, it is not composition's to keep, however long it has lived there.

## What belongs here / What must never be here

**Belongs:** deployment configuration *as data* (`DeploymentMode`, `RuntimeProfile`, `RuntimeSubstrate`, `StorageShape` — axes a deployment selects a point on, never predicates a lower crate re-derives); binding tables (`RebornHostBindings`, `RebornRuntimeInput`, `ChannelExtensionBinding`); storage backend selection (`storage_catalog.rs`); owner-factory invocation (calling `crate::owner::build_thing()` across every family, never reimplementing what it builds); readiness computation over already-constructed handles; service-graph *handles* (not the services' internal logic); background-task start/stop (the poller's *lifecycle*, not its trusted-submit *logic*); the command surface, boot wiring, and platform-service mechanics that make up a CLI; the `config.toml` schema and its seeding/validation; and the architecture test suite itself.

**Must never be here:** any domain behavior (a record shape, a state machine, a redaction rule — those belong to the family that owns the concept); policy *content* (as opposed to policy *selection*, which is fine — composition choosing "production profile" is fine, composition computing what production profile *permits* is not); prompt content (`root/default_system_prompt.rs`'s 627 lines of literal system-prompt text is exactly this, today, in composition); vendor flows beyond the two license exceptions this family shares with `product/` (the CLI's Google-OAuth resolution and gsuite/web-access registrars are grandfathered as the first-party equivalent of the extension-binding table, not a general vendor allowance); and HTTP route *handler logic* — mounting a prebuilt router behind a `host_ingress` carrier is fine, writing the handler is not (composition's `llm_admin/openai_compat_serve.rs`, 1,466 lines of actual route implementation, is this line violated today).

## Dependency direction

**Composition sees everything; nothing depends on app.** This is the two-sentence version of the whole family's dependency story and it is already half-enforced: `no_substrate_crate_depends_on_composition_root` pins the substrate half today, and PROPOSAL §11.2.1's family⇄layer consistency check makes it total in the target — no crate in `contracts/`, `substrate/`, `events/`, `domains/`, `kernel/`, `lanes/`, `loop/`, `extensions/`, or `product/` may depend on `composition`, `cli`, `config`, or `architecture`, full stop, because `app` sits at the top of the monotone ladder (`contracts < substrates < runtimes < kernel < loops < products < app`).

Internally, the family's own edges are asymmetric and worth naming precisely:

- `ironclaw_composition` has 40+ internal `ironclaw_*` path dependencies today (essentially every non-app crate in the workspace — confirmed by direct `Cargo.toml` read) plus `axum`, `tower`, `tower-http` as *external* dependencies it should not need once `llm_admin/openai_compat_serve.rs` and the webui-serve remnants shed to `openai_compat`/`operator` behind `host_ingress` carriers.
- `ironclaw_cli` depends on `ironclaw_host_api`, `ironclaw_operator`, `ironclaw_reborn_composition`, `ironclaw_reborn_config`, `ironclaw_reborn_traces`, `ironclaw_extension_host`, `ironclaw_first_party_extensions`, `ironclaw_auth`, `ironclaw_slack_extension`, `ironclaw_telegram_extension`, `ironclaw_webui` (`Cargo.toml:47-64`, confirmed) — notably it depends on `ironclaw_webui` and `ironclaw_extension_host` *directly*, not only through composition, because the serve command wires the WebChat gateway and pairing routes itself (`commands/serve.rs`, 1,732 ln).
- `ironclaw_config` depends on **nothing internal** — confirmed empty of any `ironclaw_*` line in `Cargo.toml`, external deps only (`fs4`, `serde`, `tempfile`, `thiserror`, `toml`, `toml_edit`). This "zero-workspace-dep rule" (PROPOSAL §6.10.3's closing line) is the one hard invariant this family enforces on itself, and it survives the `substrates → app` layer move unchanged — the rule was never about *layer*, it was always about *fan-out*.
- `ironclaw_architecture` depends on nothing at runtime and only `ironclaw_host_api` as a **dev-dependency** (`Cargo.toml:17`, used to import the reviewed `origin_gate_matrix` allowlist so the ratchet pins the real constant rather than a copy) — it does not *link* the crates whose boundaries it polices. Its actual enforcement mechanism is `cargo_metadata` graph introspection (parsing `cargo metadata`'s JSON output for the declared `[package.metadata.ironclaw] layer` and dependency edges) plus source-text scanning via `syn`/`proc-macro2` (both present only as dev-deps) for structural assertions like the sealed-evidence and `include_str!` scans. This is a materially different enforcement shape than every other crate in the workspace, and worth stating precisely: `ironclaw_architecture` proves things about code it never compiles into itself.

**The one open edge PROPOSAL's own text does not resolve** sits at this family's border with `product/`: `ironclaw_operator` (product family) imports `ironclaw_reborn_config::{LlmSlotSelection, RebornBootConfig}` directly today (`crates/ironclaw_operator/src/llm_admin/llm_config_service.rs:31`, a normal dependency, `Cargo.toml:26`). Once `ironclaw_config` retargets to the `app` layer, a `products`-layer crate depending on it violates the monotone matrix. See the `ironclaw_config` crate spec below and the cross-reference in `product.md`'s `ironclaw_operator` entry — this document does not invent a resolution, since PROPOSAL §6.10.3 states only that "env reads consolidate behind `ironclaw_config`" without naming who is still allowed to import the crate directly after the layer move.

## Security & authority role

**Fail-closed readiness.** `crates/ironclaw_reborn_composition/src/readiness.rs` (471 ln) is where a deployment's production/migration-dry-run profile either confirms every required handle is present or blocks — `CLAUDE.md:11` states the invariant directly: "Production and migration-dry-run profiles must fail closed on local-only or missing required handles." This is the one place in the family where "app" makes something like a security decision, and it is deliberately narrow: readiness is a gate on *whether the assembled runtime is safe to serve traffic*, never a gate on what any individual request may do (that is every downstream kernel crate's job, not this one's).

**Deployment policy as data, not content.** `deployment.rs` (1,106 ln) is where `DeploymentMode`/`RuntimeProfile`/`RuntimeSubstrate`/`StorageShape` live as plain enums a deployment *selects* — `RuntimeSubstrate::{None, ProductionShaped}` (`:87-92`) and `StorageShape::{None, LocalDevRoot, HostedSingleTenantPool, OperatorSupplied}` (`:102-111`) are axes, explicitly documented as replacing predicate functions that used to test `profile == HostedSingleTenant` or similar mode-implies-shape logic. The security property this buys: a reviewer can enumerate every deployment shape by reading four enums instead of auditing every `if profile == X` scattered through the runtime. What composition must never do — and does today, in `profile_approval_authorization.rs` (1,824 ln) and `builtin_capability_policy.rs`/`.toml` (837 ln + asset) — is compute the *content* of what a selected policy permits; that content belongs to `kernel/ironclaw_approvals`, `kernel/ironclaw_authorization`, and `kernel/ironclaw_runtime_policy`, which this family selects *between*, never authors.

**Trusted-binding tables in the binary.** Two narrow, real authority acts live in `ironclaw_cli` and nowhere else in the family. First, `native_extensions.rs`'s `ChannelExtensionBinding` table (`bundled_channel_extension_bindings()`, `native_extensions.rs:26-41`) is the sealed answer to "which concrete `ChannelAdapter` impls does this binary link" — composition receives the answer as opaque data and cannot construct it itself, by design (DEL-7). Second, `commands/serve.rs:73` implements `AdminApiTokenMinter` as `SignedSessionTokenMinter` — a port `ironclaw_reborn_composition` *defines* (an inversion: the lower-privileged assembly crate names a port, the higher-trust binary satisfies it) so that the authority to mint an admin API token is anchored in the shipped artifact, not in a library any downstream consumer could construct against. Both are single-implementor ports by design, not accidents to collapse — they exist so "who is allowed to do X" has exactly one answer, findable by reading one file.

**Architecture is the mechanism, not a participant.** Every "must never" claim this document and its `product.md` sibling make is only as real as the test that pins it. PROPOSAL §11.2 lists ten new rule classes this restructure adds — family⇄layer consistency, the zero-new-exceptions ratchet, contracts-purity allowlists, the port-location rule (e.g. `ProductSurface` definable only in `product_contracts`), the sealed-evidence rule (the `host-auth-mint` feature's deletion, pinned so it cannot silently return — directly the mechanism `product.md`'s `ironclaw_webui` migration delta depends on), the persistence-idiom rule, the cross-crate `include_str!`/`include_bytes!` scan, vendor-scope shrinkage, the dead-surface lint-flip (`unreachable_pub`/`dead_code` from opt-in warn to workspace-wide), and expanded conformance suites — and every one of them lands as a new test file inside `ironclaw_architecture`, alongside the 17 that exist today. This family's crate-vs-module argument for `ironclaw_architecture` is circular in the only acceptable way: it is a crate because its job — proving the other nine families' crate boundaries hold — cannot be a module inside any of the crates it polices without becoming unable to police itself.

## Family AGENTS.md obligations

A single `AGENTS.md` at `crates/app/` states, once: the composition charter — "wires owners, never becomes one," restated as the operative test for any change to `ironclaw_composition`'s module list; the DEL-7 binary-only-concrete-linking rule, with `native_extensions.rs` and `first_party/` named as its two enforcement sites; `ironclaw_config`'s zero-workspace-dependency rule, restated as non-negotiable regardless of its layer; and `ironclaw_architecture`'s role as mechanism rather than participant — any change that would make a boundary test *easier to pass* rather than *the underlying code more correct* is the anti-pattern this family's guidance exists to name.

`crates/AGENTS.md` — the workspace-wide, currently hand-maintained ~200-line routing table — becomes a thin family index pointing at each family root's own `AGENTS.md` (PROPOSAL §11.4's closing line). This is not cosmetic: the current file was "refreshed" 2026-07-02 and already lists six crates that no longer exist (`engine`, `tui`, `gateway`, `oauth`, `skill_learning`, `webui_v2`) plus `product_context` — seven stale references in the single guidance file most responsible for routing "where does this go" questions, and the clearest evidence in the whole audit that a flat, hand-maintained index drifts while a small set of family-scoped charters (this document among them) stay accurate because each has one family's worth of ground to cover.
