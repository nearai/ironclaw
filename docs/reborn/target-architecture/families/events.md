# Family: `crates/events/` — evidence, derived views, transport streams

**Layer(s):** `substrates` (all four crates) · **Crates (target):** 4 — `ironclaw_events`, `ironclaw_event_store` (renamed from `ironclaw_reborn_event_store`), `ironclaw_event_projections`, `ironclaw_event_streams` · **Security posture:** append-only, redacted-by-construction durable evidence feeding rebuildable, access-checked read models over a transport-neutral, admission-controlled stream — no crate in this family may decide authority, and after this pass none may write back into a durable log except `ironclaw_event_store` itself.

*Authority: PROPOSAL.md §6.3 (family role + all four crate entries), §2.6 (dead-surface list), §5 (tree), §7/§8 (trust walkthrough + dependency model), §9 rows 12–15, §12.4/§12.9/§12.10 (risks). CURRENT-state citations are to this session's read of `dde662d5a`.*

## Identity — what this family IS

`crates/events/` physicalizes PROPOSAL §6.3's three-contract split: canonical durable evidence (`ironclaw_events` + `ironclaw_event_store`) → rebuildable derived read models (`ironclaw_event_projections`) → transport-neutral delivery (`ironclaw_event_streams`). It is the system's record of what already happened, kept structurally distinct from "what a screen currently shows." One direction, four hops: a producer emits a redacted `RuntimeEvent`/audit envelope → `ironclaw_events`' traits accept it → `ironclaw_event_store` picks a durable backend and appends it under a monotonic `EventCursor` → `ironclaw_event_projections` folds the log into scoped, metadata-only read models on demand → `ironclaw_event_streams` authorizes and admits a live/replay subscription over those projections, additionally consulting `ironclaw_outbound` for push-candidate lookups. `docs/reborn/contracts/events.md` and `docs/reborn/contracts/events-projections.md` are the binding contracts this family physicalizes; this document does not amend them. CURRENTLY this is 11,970 lines across the four crates (measured this session: `events` 2,997 across 8 files, `reborn_event_store` 2,988 across 3 files, `event_projections` 3,906 across 4 files + a test submodule, `event_streams` 2,079 across 8 files). TARGET: all four retained, two narrowed by deleting verified-dead code (`events` loses its dead JSONL helpers; `event_projections` loses three dead subsystems and, as a direct consequence, two dependency edges), none merged or split.

## What makes it distinct

- **vs `substrate/`:** substrate crates (`filesystem`, `secrets`, `network`, `safety`) own timeless mechanism — containment, encryption, egress hardening — with no notion of "what happened, in order." `events/` owns the temporal evidentiary record built *on top of* substrate mechanism: `ironclaw_event_store` literally opens a `RootFilesystem` and routes durable-log operations through a `ScopedFilesystem` anchored at `/events` (`ironclaw_reborn_event_store/src/lib.rs:9-15`). The line: substrate has no history; `events/` *is* the history, and its only substrate dependency is the storage fabric it durably appends into.
- **vs `domains/`:** domain crates (`threads`, `triggers`, `memory`, …) own typed record grammar and service contracts for a *subject* — a thread, a trigger, a memory document. `events/` owns the cross-cutting fact stream domains and kernel crates emit *into*; it has no `ThreadRecord`, no `TriggerRecord`, only the redacted shape of "a thing happened" or "an audit-relevant decision was made." The line: domains model what a thing currently *is*; `events/` models what happened, replayably, independent of which domain caused it.
- **vs `product/` (projections vs product views):** `ironclaw_event_projections` produces metadata-only, replay-derived, scope-checked read models (`ThreadTimeline`, `RunStatusProjection`, `CapabilityActivityProjection`) with no write ports and no materialized store. `ironclaw_product` assembles those projections into user-facing views/DTOs and owns presentation and command semantics. The line: a projection has no opinion about how a screen renders or what a user may do next; the dependency only ever points from product down into events, never the reverse.
- **The internal three-contract split (evidence vs projection vs stream), spelled out:** `ironclaw_events` is vocabulary-and-traits with zero drivers so roughly a dozen producer crates never compile a DB/TLS stack. `ironclaw_event_store` is split out a second time purely for driver-cone isolation (`libsql`, `deadpool-postgres`, `rustls`, `tokio-postgres`, `rustls-native-certs`, `webpki-roots` — all confirmed in `Cargo.toml`) plus fail-closed production-profile policy, and depends on `ironclaw_events` rather than the reverse. `ironclaw_event_projections` is split a third time because it must stay provably non-writing — after the deletions below it depends on nothing but `ironclaw_events` + `ironclaw_host_api`. `ironclaw_event_streams` is split a fourth time because it is the *only* one of the four allowed to also see `ironclaw_outbound` (one documented method, for push-candidate lookups), a dependency none of the other three may take.

## What belongs here / What must never be here

**Belongs:** redacted event/audit vocabulary and append-log traits with no drivers (`ironclaw_events`); durable backend selection, fail-closed production-profile validation, and concrete storage adapters (`ironclaw_event_store`); replay-derived, scope/cursor-bounded read models with no materialized store (`ironclaw_event_projections`); transport-neutral subscription authorization, RAII admission, bounded live/replay stitching, and redaction validation before anything crosses a wire (`ironclaw_event_streams`).

**Must never be here:** SSE/WebSocket/webhook framing or any concrete transport (`product/ironclaw_webui`'s job); product view assembly, command handling, or presentation logic; a second write authority — nothing in `event_projections` or `event_streams` may write back into a durable log or invent state that did not come from replaying one; vendor names; raw secrets, raw host paths, raw tool I/O, approval reasons, invocation fingerprints, or lease material in any persisted or streamed shape — the redaction invariant `ironclaw_events`' constructors enforce at the source (`lib.rs:12-35`).

## Dependency direction

`ironclaw_events` depends on `ironclaw_host_api` only — verified via `Cargo.toml`, the leaf of the family. `ironclaw_event_store` depends on `ironclaw_events`, `ironclaw_filesystem`, `ironclaw_host_api` (+ `ironclaw_common` as a dev-dependency). `ironclaw_event_projections` depends on `ironclaw_events`, `ironclaw_host_api` in the target; today it also names `ironclaw_memory` and `ironclaw_turns`, and both edges are verified to be justified only by the dead code this same move deletes (below). `ironclaw_event_streams` depends on `ironclaw_event_projections`, `ironclaw_host_api`, `ironclaw_outbound` (one documented method), and turn vocabulary through `ironclaw_host_api` in the target — today a direct `ironclaw_turns` dependency, one of the seven `W4.3` `LAYER_MATRIX_EXCEPTIONS` (`reborn_dependency_boundaries.rs:3516` ff., the `ironclaw_event_streams`/`ironclaw_turns` entry confirmed live in that table). Nothing outside this family depends *into* it except through `events`'/`event_projections`' public traits and DTOs; the family never depends on `kernel/`, `loop/`, `extensions/`, or `product/`. The `app/ironclaw_composition → ironclaw_event_streams` `Cargo.toml` edge is verified unused (zero references to `ironclaw_event_streams` anywhere under `ironclaw_reborn_composition/src/`) and is dropped in the same pass.

## Security & authority role

This family holds two of the kernel-listed responsibilities from `docs/reborn/contracts/kernel-boundary.md` — "durable audit/event append + replay cursors" — and enforces `docs/reborn/contracts/events-projections.md`'s rule verbatim: "projections rebuildable, never authority." In PROPOSAL §7's trust walkthrough this family sits at **T7** (lane outcome → safe evidence: redaction/output-limit/leak checks complete, durable typed events + audit envelopes append) and feeds **PROJ** (event_store → event_projections → product, "safe-to-project ... scope-checked, redaction-validated, rebuildable" — a guarantee this family's dead-code deletions below make mechanically true, not merely contractual). `event_streams` additionally enforces the **watch-vs-push separation**: subscription (watch) authorization is a decision distinct from `outbound`'s push authorization, and `event_streams` itself never sends — it only authorizes, admits, and reads push candidates.

## Family AGENTS.md obligations

Per §11.4, `crates/events/AGENTS.md` is written when the family directory first exists and must carry, verbatim or by direct reference, the line PROPOSAL §6.3 states for this family: **"projections never write authority; streams never invent state; store isolates drivers."** Three of the four crates already carry this discipline piecemeal in their own guidance (`ironclaw_event_projections/AGENTS.md` + `CLAUDE.md`, `ironclaw_event_streams/AGENTS.md` + `CLAUDE.md`, `ironclaw_events/AGENTS.md` + `CLAUDE.md`); `ironclaw_event_store` is the one gap — it has an `AGENTS.md` but no `CLAUDE.md` (verified this session: only `AGENTS.md` exists under `crates/ironclaw_reborn_event_store/`), and that `AGENTS.md` is exactly where the stale feature-gating claim lives (see its crate spec above). Both gaps are fixed as part of the family root's guidance pass, per §11.4's observation that guidance-presence correlates with discipline. The family root must also state the one cross-crate rule no single crate's local guide can state alone: the four crates form a one-way pipeline (evidence → store → projection → stream), and a dependency arrow pointing backward through that list is always wrong regardless of what any individual crate's local rules permit.
